Orchestration

Orchestration, here, means several developers, each directing their own AI agents, working towards one goal: they plan the work, split it, build it in parallel and merge it, through one shared record.

Today, one developer, Andrius, directs one orchestrator: a Claude session that plans the work, sends out agents to do it and checks what they report. That part is how Orion-81 is made, and it works. How two developers could share the project in the same way is a proposal for discussion, not current practice. Each section below says which it is.

The working loop

Every batch of work runs the same seven steps

1

Plan

Opus: items, owners, checks

2

Execute

Sonnet, in parallel

3

Integrate

Sonnet: merge, build, time

4

Review

Opus: find and judge

5

Fix

Sonnet: each finding

6

Verify

Sonnet: run the checks

7

Accept

Opus: sign off, close

Blue steps are planner work (Opus 5.5); sand steps are executor work (Sonnet 5.5). Steps 5 and 6 repeat until the review is clean.

Example, 28 October: M2.3’s part M was built by a Sonnet builder, reviewed by two Opus reviewers, fixed in two rounds and re-checked, then merged and timed at 63.52 ms against your 70 ms limit.

Spec-driven chunks

From your idea to main in four stages

1 · Spec

Clean-room writers turn your ideas into spec text. Lint, isolated scans and sanitisers check it. You sign.

Hands on: signed specs, each file pinned by its hash

2 · Plan

A planner cross-checks the plan against the code it builds on: frozen contracts, work items, file scopes, a test-time budget.

Hands on: the cross-check and its tracker items

3 · Build

Waves of items on the milestone’s own integration branch. Each item owns its files; review, fix, re-check.

Hands on: a merged, timed integration branch

4 · Land

The close: four builds from clean, lint, provenance scans, a planner review. Then the merge into main, on your word.

Hands on: a new main and a version number

M2.3, end to end: batches A2 and B signed on 27 October → cross-check revisions 1 to 12 → parts C, M and wave 4 merged → its spec batch, close and merge to come (O81-0367 to O81-0369).

Rules of engagement

Six rules for working side by side

1 · One owner per folder

A file has one writer at a time; every tracker item names its files.

2 · Contracts move by item

Interfaces are frozen in the cross-check; changing one is a planner item both sides see.

3 · One merge at a time

Milestones land in main in the planned order, each with full evidence and your word.

4 · Re-check first

Before building, each side cross-checks its plan against the latest main.

5 · Review each other

At every close, the other side’s fresh Opus reviewer is the independent read.

6 · One reference clock

Test times are binding on one machine only; every log time comes from the system clock.

Your questions: one queue for both sides, each question with an explainer page checked before it reaches you.

The one-line version

Split by contract. Build in parallel. Merge in order. Review each other.
